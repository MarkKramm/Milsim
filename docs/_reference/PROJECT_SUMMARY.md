---

## What this game is

A living military base simulation where personnel act autonomously, systems
create persistent consequences, and the player commands **by exception** —
setting policy, inspecting, and intervening only when a decision matters.

The player never micromanages. They watch, understand why things happen, and
make a small number of meaningful choices.

**The single question the prototype must answer:**

> Is it fun to watch a small autonomous base operate, understand why things
> happen, and occasionally change a policy?

Everything else is downstream of that.

## The eight decisions that shape everything

1. **Stakes** — a readiness number that drifts down when people are hungry,
   tired, or idle; at zero, people quit. Injuries come later.
2. **Agency** — decisions are concrete named options, not sliders. They queue
   in the report rather than interrupting.
3. **Normal days** — small anomalies generated by rules, never scripted story.
4. **Pacing** — 1 tick = 1 in-game minute, 8 ticks/sec at 1×, so an in-game day
   is ~3 real minutes. Speeds: pause / 1× / 4× / 16×.
5. **Stack** — TypeScript + Vite + React, running in a phone browser.
6. **Architecture** — the simulation is a pure module with no UI imports,
   runnable headless.
7. **Determinism** — one seeded random stream from tick zero. No `Math.random()`
   in simulation.
8. **No grid** — panels only. No map required.

---

## The single most important rule

> The simulation contains no UI code. If a React component contains a rule
> about how the world works, that is a bug.

This is what makes the game testable, reproducible, saveable, and debuggable
headlessly. It is enforced by `tools/headless.ts`, which must keep working
forever.

## Scope: what is in, what is out

**In the prototype:** 6–8 people, hunger and fatigue, food and maintenance
supplies, a few jobs, readiness, decisions, a report, and a timeline.

**Deliberately not yet:** combat, raids, vehicles, animation, 3D, pathfinding,
grids, recruitment, skill trees, faction warfare.

None of those are permanently banned — they are **sequenced**. See the table in
`LOCKED_DECISIONS.md` for what unlocks each one and when.

## The plan in brief

Build boring stages first, in order, and stop at each gate until it genuinely
passes:

1. **State advances** — a clock and numbers. One evening.
2. **People work** — they choose tasks autonomously and show their reason.
3. **Needs and consequence** — something can be lost.
4. **Decisions and reports** — you choose, and you understand the effect.
5. **Long-term play** — you want to play day 5.

Only then expand, one layer at a time.

---

## The most likely way this project fails

Not rushing — **drifting**. A passion project with no deadline dies when months
go into systems that were never played. The defense is the gates: you cannot
advance past a stage that is not yet interesting, however much you want to build
the factory layer.

The second most likely failure is building the factory/production layer before
anyone has confirmed that watching six people eat and sleep is entertaining. In a
simulation, you cannot design the interesting systems until the boring ones are
proven interesting.

## If you are an AI assistant picking this up

- The design is settled. Do not re-litigate `LOCKED_DECISIONS.md`.
- The most useful thing you can do is **help build Stage 1 exactly as specified**,
  or to critique whether the current gate is genuinely met.
- If you think a decision is wrong, say so plainly and explain the cost — but do
  not silently change scope.
- The player is a beginner/intermediate developer. Explain unfamiliar tooling
  rather than assuming it.
